# The numbers, in the open.
SpamHelper explores card potential with a deterministic expected-value model.
## Cards
Card tiers are relative to the catalogue: S is the top 8%, A the top 25%, B the top 50%, and C the top 75%. The score combines attack, generation, defense, support, control, and economy. A role marks a genuine strength: a card only gets one when it ranks among the best in the catalogue on that axis. A card without a clear strength is versatile if it holds up on several axes, and utility otherwise.
## Decks
Decks are found through beam search, followed by talent optimization. The relative index compares their score with an average random deck and the best deck found. S ≥ 90%, A ≥ 80%, B ≥ 70%, C ≥ 50%. It is not a win rate.
## Theoretical analysis
A 10-turn model. The displayed gameplan and metrics are simulated against a neutral opponent (4 cards with 700 HP and 10% resistance in every element). The four enemy cards each have their own health: an area attack spreads its damage across targets instead of stacking it, and damage beyond a card’s remaining health is lost. The opponent strikes back with the cards still standing: eliminating early reduces incoming damage for the rest of the game; each of your cards has its own health and falls when it runs out, and a taunt draws the blows onto itself. No real meta can be observed: a deck’s score is the average of its scores against nine theoretical opponent profiles (neutral, fragile, tank, six decks resisting one element), each played first and second, and the gap against each profile gives its strengths and weaknesses. One active talent per card, chosen before the match; the "no talent" reading ranks the same decks for a player without duplicates. Ability order is heuristic. The Tox plan follows the deck’s Spamtons and the rarities offered as turns go by; draws remain random. Real matches can differ.
